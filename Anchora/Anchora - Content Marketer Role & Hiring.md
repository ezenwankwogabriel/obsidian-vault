# Anchora — Content Marketer Role & Hiring Guide

**Purpose:** Define the ONE content hire Anchora needs right now, what they own, how to screen them, and how to onboard them without breaking positioning or compliance rules.

**Stage:** Pre/early-launch (Sprint 9). Solo founder. No content team. No design budget.
**Recommendation:** Hire ONE fractional Content Marketer (contract, ~10–15 hrs/wk) before any other content role.

---

## 1. The role, in one sentence

A **fractional Content Marketer** who owns strategy *and* production *and* measurement — someone who asks "how does this piece get a Nigerian professional to open the product," not "how many words do you want."

### Why this role (not these others — yet)

| Don't hire first | Why not |
|---|---|
| Content Writer | Produces output, not outcomes. 10 blog posts/month, zero signups. |
| Social Media Manager | Manages posting cadence, not the *why* behind the content. No funnel ownership. |
| SEO/Growth Marketer | Needed, but only after problem-education content shows real search intent. |
| Video editor / designer | Production capacity before you've validated *what* to produce. |

All four come later (see hiring order in the GTM stack). The Content Marketer can source freelance writers/editors as needed and manage them.

---

## 2. What they own (the job)

**Four jobs, one person:**

1. **Strategy** — turn the 4-pillar GTM into a weekly content plan mapped to the funnel (see [[Anchora - Content-to-Funnel Map]]). Every piece has a job and a CTA.
2. **Production** — write/edit posts, threads, explainers, and short-form scripts. Produce at least one long-form (blog/guide) per week for search, plus the short-form derivatives.
3. **Distribution** — post to X, Instagram, TikTok, and make content WhatsApp-forwardable (the dominant share channel in Nigeria).
4. **Measurement** — weekly report on funnel movement (signups, waitlist, account activations, not impressions/likes).

### Responsibilities mapped to the 4 pillars

| Pillar | % | What the hire does | Who signs off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Living Value Dashboard** | 35% | Product walkthroughs, "organize your records" how-tos, feature-in-use content | Gabby (positioning) |
| **Problem Education** | 25% | "What happens to your accounts when you're not around" content; searchable blog | Gabby (compliance) |
| **Founder Building** | 25% | *Ghostwrites/edits for Gabby* — the voice stays Gabby's | Gabby (final) |
| **Product Updates** | 15% | Changelog-to-content, feature drop posts, screenshots | Gabby (accuracy) |

**Note:** The hire does NOT own the founder voice. They support it. Founder Building is Gabby's pillar; the hire drafts, Gabby approves and posts (or posts on Gabby's behalf with approval).

## 3. Non-negotiables (screening criteria)

These are dealbreakers. Screen for them explicitly — they are unusual constraints most content hires have never faced.

1. **Compliance fluency.** Must be able to write about a digital legacy product while *never* using: "estate," "beneficiary," "executor," "recovery" (user-facing), "manage," "credential handover," "financial planning," "wealth management," or "asset transfer." This is a record-keeping + guided-discovery tool, not an estate/finance product.
2. **Living-benefit-first.** Content leads with "get your records organized *today*." The legacy angle is the *reveal*, never the headline. A hire who defaults to death-scarcity framing ("what if you die tomorrow") is the wrong hire.
3. **Nigerian-market fluency.** Understands WhatsApp as the primary share surface, the institutional knowledge moat (PFA/PenCom, bank next-of-kin procedures, NGX/CHN identifiers), and how trust is built in this market.
4. **Funnel-first thinking.** Measures success in signups/activations, not impressions. Can articulate the attribution path from a piece of content to an action.
5. **Correct user-facing label discipline.** "Trusted Contact" (or "Trusted Person"), never "beneficiary" or "next-of-kin" as the product term.

---

## 4. Requirements

**Must-have**
- 2+ years content marketing with demonstrable funnel results (a piece that drove signups/purchases, and *how they know* it was the content)
- Strong writing in Nigerian English (code-switching naturally between formal and Pidgin-adjacent where appropriate, without forcing it)
- Comfort producing both long-form (SEO blog) and short-form (X threads, Instagram/TikTok scripts)
- Self-serve measurement (UTM discipline, basic analytics, signup-source tracking)

**Nice-to-have**
- Fintech/SaaS experience (not required — a generalist who thinks funnel-first beats a fintech writer who doesn't)
- Short-form video production (scripting + editing with CapCut/Canva)
- Existing distribution in the Nigerian creator/tech space

---

## 5. Compensation (starting point — validate against local rates)

Fractional, contract, Naira or USD depending on the hire:

- **₦150,000 – ₦400,000/month** for ~10–15 hrs/wk (Nigerian local hire), or
- **$400 – $1,200/month** (remote/global fractional)

Tie a portion of compensation to a **funnel outcome** (e.g. X signups/month or waitlist growth), not to post count. This filters for funnel-first candidates immediately.

## 6. Hiring process (fast, no committees)

1. **Written screen (30 min, async):** Give them a 20-minute task — write 3 headlines + a 5-tweet thread for a record-keeping product that leads with living benefit and uses zero compliance-banned words. (This is the single highest-signal filter — see Question 4 below.)
2. **45-min call:** the 5 interview questions below.
3. **Paid trial (2 weeks, small scope):** have them run one pillar end-to-end (e.g. produce one week of Living Value content + one SEO piece) and measure signup-source. Hire or don't based on real output.

---

## 7. Interview questions (with "strong answer" signals)

**Q1. Walk me through a piece of content you produced that directly led to signups or purchases. How did you know it was the content, and not something else?**
*Strong answer:* Names the piece, the source-tracking method (UTM, promo code, dedicated landing page, waitlist form), the number, and the time window. Admits attribution is fuzzy but shows a real method. *Weak:* "Our engagement went up a lot" or "I just write good content, sales handles the rest."

**Q2. Our product's living value is 'organize where your financial and physical assets are held — today.' The legacy angle ('what happens when you're gone') is a reveal, not the headline. Sketch how you'd structure a post that opens with the living benefit but lands the deeper question.**
*Strong answer:* Starts with an immediate, self-interested hook (declutter, peace of mind, "you don't actually know where everything is right now"), builds tension, then pivots to "and that's why the 'what happens later' question matters." Shows they can hold both framings without leading with death. *Weak:* Leads with mortality/loss, or can't articulate the pivot.

**Q3. Our audience is Nigerian professionals and entrepreneurs. Which channels and formats would you start with, and why — give me specific accounts or content styles in this market you'd model.**
*Strong answer:* Names WhatsApp as the share surface, picks 1–2 primary channels (not "everywhere"), cites real Nigerian creators/accounts by name, explains *why* their format works for this audience. *Weak:* Recites the global SaaS playbook (LinkedIn + SEO + newsletter) with zero Nigeria-specific references.

**Q4. Write for a record-keeping product that can never say 'estate,' 'beneficiary,' 'executor,' 'recovery,' or 'wealth management.' Give me three trust-building headlines under those constraints.**
*Strong answer:* Produces three compliant, compelling headlines on the spot — e.g. around "know where everything is," "your people won't have to guess," "a map of your financial life." *Weak:* Freezes, or smuggles in a banned word ("secure your estate"), or produces generic "we keep your records safe" fluff.

**Q5. How do you measure whether content is working? Take me past impressions and likes.**
*Strong answer:* Names a north-star conversion event (signup, activation, waitlist), then the leading indicators that predict it (CTR on CTA, signup-source by piece, activation rate of referred users), and how they'd close the loop weekly. *Weak:* Stops at reach, engagement rate, follower growth.

---

## 8. Red flags (walk away)

- Can't give a single concrete example of content → outcome with attribution.
- Defaults to fear/death-scarcity framing when asked to sell a legacy product.
- Proposes "estate planning" / "beneficiary" / "wealth management" language unthinkingly.
- No Nigeria-specific distribution plan (the market is WhatsApp + trust, not a generic funnel).
- Negotiates only on post count ("I can do 30 posts/month") rather than on outcomes.

## 10. What Gabby keeps (never outsourced)

- **Founder voice and final sign-off** on all Founder Building content
- **Positioning and compliance** — final call on any copy that touches the category
- **Product truth** — the hire never describes features that don't exist or aren't shipped
- **High-trust relationships** — media, partnerships, and key community members stay with Gabby

The hire multiplies Gabby's output; they don't replace Gabby as the face of Anchora.
