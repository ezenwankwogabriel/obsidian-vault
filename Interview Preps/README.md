# Behavioral Interview Prep

A structured system for acing "tell me about a time…" questions.

> **Folder contents:** Behavioral prep (this file, [[Question Bank]], [[Story Bank Worksheet]]) + [[JS preps - var let const]] (JavaScript technical prep).

## What these questions are

Behavioral (also called **competency-based**) questions. The interviewer isn't asking for a story — they're collecting **evidence of a specific competency**. The underlying assumption: **past behavior predicts future performance.**

Every behavioral question maps to one of ~8 competencies (see [[Question Bank]]). They are all answered with the same structure: **STAR**.

## The STAR method

| Step | What it is | Time budget (of ~2 min) |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | 1–2 sentences of context. Specific, tight. | ~15% |
| **T**ask | What *you* were responsible for. Use "I", not "we". | ~15% |
| **A**ction | What *you* did, in sequence. **The meat.** | ~50% |
| **R**esult | The outcome, **quantified** (number, %, date, time saved). | ~20% |

Add **L** (Learning) — one sentence on what you'd do differently — for "failure" and "challenge" questions. Interviewers actively want it there; leaving it out reads as "has never reflected."

## The 3 most common failure modes

1. **Context dump** — 70% of the answer spent on Situation. Cut it to 2 sentences.
2. **"We" instead of "I"** — the interviewer needs to hear *your* actions. Say "I".
3. **Vague results** — "it went well" is a non-answer. Give a number, a date, a percentage.

## How to use this folder

1. **Read** [[Question Bank]] to see the full landscape.
2. **Build** your story bank in [[Story Bank Worksheet]] — 8–12 stories, each mapped to the competencies it can serve.
3. **Practice** each story out loud until it lands in 90–120 seconds.

## The day before the interview

- [ ] Pick your 6 strongest stories and re-read them
- [ ] For each, confirm the Result has at least one number
- [ ] Say your "challenge" story out loud twice
- [ ] Prepare 2–3 smart questions to ask *them*
